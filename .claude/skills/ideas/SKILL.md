Ideas the human brought, if any: $ARGUMENTS

Generate real candidates, score them honestly, and kill the bad ones — including the human's own. They can come up with horrible plans and have asked to be told. This runs once, at kickoff, on the bigger model, until K+0:40 (PLAYBOOK Phase 1; the `Now:` line is the clock). At most 3 AskUserQuestion calls: the interview, the pick, one re-pick after a regenerate; the sponsor-list ask is plain text. Exempt from the one-question and show-something rules per CLAUDE.md → How to read me (last bullet).

0. SPEC.md exists, or PHASE ≥ 2 → change nothing: "/ideas is kickoff-only — the idea is already chosen (PHASE n)." CLAUDE.md → Idea already filled (a second run) → it is a candidate too, and the `RUNNER-UP` line gets replaced, not appended.
1. PHASE 0 → do PLAYBOOK Phase 0 first. Read CLAUDE.md → "Sponsor / company challenges to target" and Stack, and the "what wins here" paragraph in `.claude/skills/spec/SKILL.md` — the lens for everything below. The sponsor list says `not released yet` (PLAYBOOK Phase 0) → score sponsor fit as unknown and name the card most likely to change when it lands. The Timeline needs no question: PLAYBOOK → Knowing where we are assumes K and E.
2. **Interview: one AskUserQuestion, three questions, no recommendation, options in no particular order** — a tired person clicks the first thing.
   - Q1, the signal. If `$ARGUMENTS` names a domain, dig into it: "On a shift/in that class/doing that, what goes wrong or wastes time?" Otherwise: "What annoyed you or someone near you this month?" with options that are kinds of answer — my job · my classes · a hobby · someone I look after — and the text "pick Other and type the actual thing; the options are only prompts".
   - Q2, multi-select facts: first hackathon · phone at the table · can print a prop · own sample data or files.
   - Q3, what they'd rather demo, as what the judge sees: the judge hands over something and gets a result · the screen reacts live to the judge · a before/after · a map, timeline, or chart fills in.
3. **Generate 8–10 one-line candidates internally, then cull to 4 cards** (≤ 100 words each) plus one line each, at most 4, under "also considered — cut because …". Every idea the human brought (`$ARGUMENTS`, or named in an answer) is an extra card, scored and searched like the rest; a vague one is made concrete from their Q1 answer, with "read as: …" on the card. Rules:
   - Build only from what CLAUDE.md → Stack and "How this codebase is wired" already provide, plus pure-Python libraries (live updates = polling). That list is a constraint, not a menu: at least one card has no photo input. Nothing that needs hardware, scraping, a paid API, real people's private data, third-party approval, visiting untrusted URLs, or a dataset you can't produce in an hour.
   - Before writing a card, name the project you'd expect in any hackathon gallery for that problem. If your pitch sentence describes that project too, add a specific twist or drop the candidate.
   - The technical piece must be something a chat window can't do: a decode, a measurement, a cross-check, a real-time loop, a transformation. "Gemini looks at it and explains" alone is out.
   - The judge moment is watched within about 10 seconds of the judge's own action, never explained, using only the props Q2 says the human has. A phone step only works against the deployed URL — the table demo is localhost — so a card that needs one names that risk or uses the laptop webcam.
   - Sponsors: `/spec`'s sponsor rule (its step 2). A theme track is claimed only with the sentence "fits X because the judge watches Y"; an API track only if it is the heart of the idea and its free key is obtainable at the event in 10 minutes; eligibility tracks (first-time hacker) go on every eligible card and never break ties. Never move a piece onto the sponsor's API when plain code does it more reliably — losing a track is cheaper than losing Completion. Two strong fits if the list allows it without forcing; if it doesn't, say so.
   Card format: **Name** — one-sentence pitch · For: … · Judge watches: … · Hard part: … · Sponsors: … (one "because" each) · Skeleton by K+10 (≈ 9 hours of building): yes / risky — what sinks it.
4. **Score every card on the five judging criteria, 1–5.** Completion: buildable by K+10 by shcht with the human answering questions — a card that says "risky" caps at 3; more than 3 new screens on the demo path, or an untried library, is a 2; 1–2 is out regardless of total. Originality: blank until step 5. Design: 5 = the judge reacts within 10 seconds of their own action, 3 = watched but flat, 1 = explained. Technology: 2 = CRUD or one model call, 3 = a model call plus deterministic processing the demo depends on, 4 = a measurement or cross-check the model can't do alone, 5 = a real-time loop or vision running under the judge's hands. Practicality: 5 = the named person would switch from what they use today, 3 = they'd try it once, 1 = nobody.
5. **Originality search, at most 5 minutes:** one search per card, including the human's (Devpost galleries — ShellHacks and similar — and the obvious products), then a second search for the top 2 by the other four scores (ties: Completion). WebSearch results are enough; WebFetch only on devpost.com pages — anything else prompts. Originality: 1 = a direct match exists, 5 = no match for the twist. A card whose twist already exists is out. Say what exists and what is different.
6. **Table and verdict:** all cards with totals; ties: more claimed tracks (eligibility tracks excluded), then Originality. Recommend one, with its single biggest risk and the cheapest mitigation (a committed sample input in `backend/demo/`, a manual fallback path). One line each on what is out and why. One line on tracks skipped and why — hardware is out for this starter; "if boards are handed out at the table, say so and I'll rescore". Fewer than two cards survive → step 7's first option is regenerate.
7. **Pick — one AskUserQuestion, two questions.** (a) Which to build: the recommended card first; the runner-up (the best card that isn't out — say if that differs from the score order); combine two, only if the combination beats both and you say how; regenerate, with what to change. (b) "Which would you rather explain to ten judges?" — the top two in random order, no recommendation. If (a) and (b) disagree and the scores are within 2 points, (b) wins. Regenerate once at most, then a re-pick; "you pick" means the top score.
8. Write CLAUDE.md → Idea per its placeholder, including the judge moment; mark the claimed tracks in the Sponsor list with `← claimed`; write (or replace) `RUNNER-UP (not built): …` in Decisions — PLAYBOOK Phase 2 and `/spec` say when it's used. Don't commit; `/spec` does. Then say: "type `/spec <the idea in one sentence>` — same model, no `/clear`."
