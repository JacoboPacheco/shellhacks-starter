Ideas the human brought, if any: $ARGUMENTS

Generate real candidates, score them honestly, and kill the bad ones — including the human's own. They can come up with horrible plans and have asked to be told. The verdict is advice: if they override it, say the single biggest risk once, then build theirs. Runs at kickoff, on the bigger model, until K+0:40 (PLAYBOOK Phase 1; the `Now:` line is the clock) — past K+0:40, regenerate is no longer offered. At most 3 AskUserQuestion calls: the interview, the pick, one re-pick after a regenerate. Exempt from the one-question and show-something rules per CLAUDE.md → How to read me (last bullet).

0. SPEC.md exists, or PHASE ≥ 2 → change nothing: "/ideas is kickoff-only — the idea is already chosen (PHASE n)." CLAUDE.md → Idea already filled (a second run) → it is a candidate too, and the `RUNNER-UP` line gets replaced, not appended.
1. PHASE 0 → do PLAYBOOK Phase 0 first. Read CLAUDE.md → "Sponsor / company challenges to target" and Stack, and in `.claude/skills/spec/SKILL.md` the "what wins here" paragraph (above its step 0) and the sponsor rule with its claim tests (step 2) — the lens for everything below. The sponsor list says `not released yet` (PLAYBOOK Phase 0) → write `Sponsors: unknown` on every card, skip step 6's no-honest-card line, and name the card most likely to change when it lands. The Timeline needs no question: PLAYBOOK → Knowing where we are assumes K and E.
2. **Interview: one AskUserQuestion, three or four questions, no recommendation, options in no particular order** — a tired person clicks the first thing.
   - Q1, the signal. Per idea the human brought (up to two), one question digging into its domain: "Doing that, what goes wrong or wastes time?" with options a slow step · a common mistake · something tracked by hand · something nobody can see. No idea brought: "What annoyed you or someone near you this month?" with options my job · my classes · a hobby · something at home. Either way the text says "pick Other and type the actual thing; the options are only prompts".
   - Q2, multi-select facts: first hackathon · phone at the table · can print a prop · own sample data or files.
   - Q3, what they'd rather demo, as what the judge sees: the judge gives it an input and gets a result · the screen reacts live to the judge · a before/after · a map, timeline, or chart fills in. At least two surviving cards use the shape they pick.
3. **Generate 8–10 one-line candidates internally, then cull to 4 cards** (≤ 100 words each) plus one line each, at most 4, under "also considered — cut because …". Every idea the human brought (`$ARGUMENTS`, or named in an answer) is an extra card, scored and searched like the rest; a vague one is made concrete from their Q1 answer, with "read as: …" on the card; a generated candidate that is their idea with a twist goes on their card as "twist: …", not a separate one. Rules:
   - Build only from what CLAUDE.md → Stack and "How this codebase is wired" already provide, plus libraries that pip-install from wheels on Windows and Linux with no system install (live updates = polling). That list is a constraint, not a menu: at least one card has no photo input, and no more than two generated cards share an input type. Nothing that needs hardware, scraping, a paid API, real people's private data, third-party approval, visiting untrusted URLs, or a dataset you can't produce in an hour.
   - Before writing a card, name the project you'd expect in any hackathon gallery for that problem — it goes on the card as the gallery default. If your pitch sentence describes that project too, add a specific twist or drop the candidate.
   - The technical piece must be something a chat window can't do: a decode, a measurement, a cross-check, a live loop, a transformation. "Gemini looks at it and explains" alone is out.
   - Demo format: the "what wins here" paragraph. The judge moment is watched within about 10 seconds of the judge's own action, never explained, using only the props Q2 says the human has; a card that needs a phone names that risk or uses the laptop webcam.
   - Sponsors: `/spec`'s sponsor rule and its claim tests, applied per card. Coverage is a result, not a goal — never generate a card to cover a track.
   Card format: **Name** — one-sentence pitch · For: … · Judge watches: … · Hard part: … · Gallery default: … · Sponsors: … (one "because" each, or none) · Skeleton by K+10 (≈ 9 hours of building): yes / risky — what sinks it.
4. **Score every card on the five judging criteria, 1–5.** Completion: buildable by K+10 by shcht with the human answering questions — a card that says "risky" caps at 3; a sponsor API is risky, not untried; more than 3 new screens on the demo path, or a library that needs a system install or native build, is a 2; 1–2 is out regardless of total. Originality: blank until step 5. Design: 5 = the judge reacts within 10 seconds of their own action, 4 = reacts after a one-sentence setup, 3 = watched but flat, 2 = watched only after more than a sentence of setup, 1 = explained. Technology: 2 = CRUD or one model call (one frame or file sent to a model is 2 or 3), 3 = a model call, threshold, or reformat that the demo depends on, 4 = a measurement or cross-check the model can't do alone, 5 = the judge's own input drives a non-trivial computation the model can't do (a fit, a solve, a search, a cross-check), whatever the input. Practicality: 5 = the named person would switch from what they use today, 3 = they'd try it once, 1 = nobody; a legal or compliance blocker for that person caps it at 3.
5. **Originality search, at most 5 minutes:** two queries per card not already out on Completion, including the human's — `site:devpost.com <gallery default>` and `<gallery default> app` — then one query on the twist for the top 2 surviving cards by the other four scores (ties: Completion). WebSearch results are enough; WebFetch only on devpost.com pages — anything else prompts. Originality: 1 = a direct match exists, 3 = the problem is well covered and the twist is small, 4 = nothing relevant found, 5 = results exist for the problem but none has the twist. A card whose twist already exists, or that is worse than a free tool the named person already has, is out. Say what exists and what is different.
6. **Table and verdict:** all cards with totals; ties: Originality, then Completion, then the card using Q3's shape. Recommend one, with its single biggest risk and the cheapest mitigation (a committed sample input in `backend/demo/`, a manual fallback path). One line each on what is out and why. One line on tracks with no honest card, and why (hardware, if the list has such a track, is out for this starter). After a regenerate that changed the tone, if the new top card scores below the old one, say so in one line.
7. **Pick — one AskUserQuestion, two questions.** (a) Which to build: the recommended card first; the runner-up (the best surviving card that isn't the recommendation — say if that differs from the score order); combine two, only if the combination beats both — it is scored like a card before it's offered, and you say how; regenerate ("pick Other and type what to change"), offered only before K+0:40 and only if unused. (b) "Which would you rather explain to ten judges?" — the top two surviving cards in random order, no recommendation. (b) is a check, not a vote: if it names a different card than (a), say one plain line — "You picked X but would rather explain Y — building X; say 'Y' to switch" — and build X; a regenerate or combine answer ignores (b). In either round, fewer than two survivors → (b) is skipped and (a) offers the survivors, then the best "out" cards with their reasons, up to three, plus regenerate if available — always 2–4 options; zero survivors → recommend the highest total and state its killer and mitigation. **Regenerate** reruns steps 3–6 with the change applied on top of the Q answers: earlier cards are out unless the human names one, one query per new card and no twist query, no also-considered lines, the runner-up comes from the new round. The re-pick's (a) has no regenerate option; "you pick", "none", or another regenerate means the top surviving card of the latest round — say so.
8. Write CLAUDE.md → Idea per its placeholder, including the judge moment, and put the name in `# Project: [NAME]`; mark the claimed tracks in the Sponsor list with `← claimed` (keep the full list); write (or replace) `RUNNER-UP (not built): <name> — <pitch> · Judge watches: … · Sponsors: …` in Decisions — the best surviving card that isn't the one picked; none → no line. PLAYBOOK Phase 2 and `/spec` say when it's used. Append `Facts: <Q2 answers>` to Decisions (`/spec` and `/pitch` read it). If the human overrides the pick later, rewrite all of this in place.
